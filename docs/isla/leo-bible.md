# Leo: story bible (draft, no scripted lines yet)

Everything the writing must stay true to. Checked against `episode-rules.md` before any script is written. Content stays everyday and non-graphic: nobody in this family is at fault, and nothing here is a mystery to solve. Items marked **(decision)** are choices that can change.

## 1. Who he is

**Leo**, seven and a half (birthday in August). Second grade. Tall for his age, missing a bottom tooth that already grew back crooked, with one untied shoelace most afternoons. Unlike Wren, he is loud, fast-talking, bossy in play, and immediately puts adults to work in whatever game he has invented.

**What a seven-and-a-half-year-old can and cannot do** (the writing depends on this):
- Talks in brisk, declarative commands when playing ("You're the nurse. Write that down.") and uses "actually" and "basic'ly" when explaining rules.
- Understands that death is permanent (unlike a four-year-old), and has just discovered the terrifying corollary: **if one grown-up in the family died, other grown-ups can die too.**
- Takes adult figures of speech literally. If an adult says "Pop's body got very tired, so he went to sleep," then **being very tired + closing your eyes = dying.** If Mom says "I'm dead tired," that is an emergency.
- Measures age by visible clues he can count: **white hairs, wrinkles around the eyes, knees that crack when standing up, yawning after dinner.** He does not know his parents' exact ages.
- Hates being treated like a baby or asked "feeling" questions. If an adult steps out of the game to ask if he is scared, he fires them from the game or makes a loud, silly joke.
- Can stay inside a pretend frame for a long time and tell the whole truth through his "patients" as long as the adult stays in character with him.

## 1b. Leo as a person **(decision)**

He was always a busy, bossy, rule-inventing boy long before December. Pop's death did not make him bossy; it gave his bossiness a job he thinks he cannot put down.

**Temperament.** Energetic, observant, literal, funny on purpose. In first grade he ran a "lost and found detective agency" at recess that mostly consisted of handing people rocks.

**Habits**
- Unclips and re-clips a **tiny red plastic squeeze light** on his jacket zipper when he is thinking.
- Listens to his own chest or other people's chests, counting under his breath.
- **Counts his mom's white hairs** when she leans down to tie his shoes or kiss him goodnight.
- Pokes his mom awake whenever she closes her eyes on the couch after work, usually by dropping a Lego bin or asking her how to spell a long word.
- Leaves his bedroom door open two inches at night so he can see down the hall.
- Says "Write that down" whenever he wants an adult to take him seriously.

**What he is good at.** Remembering dinosaur names and what they ate. Drawing maps with arrows. Whistling through the gap in his teeth. Spotting when a grown-up is yawning behind their hand.

**What makes him laugh.** Sound effects. Giving stuffed animals ridiculous medical diagnoses ("His tail has the flops"). Adults who take his pretend orders seriously and say "Yes, Doctor."

**Colors.** Bright **red** (like fire trucks and his squeeze light) and **green** (like the numbers on the stove clock).

**Food.** Pretzel sticks (which he uses as "thermometers" before eating them). Apple slices, but only if the peel is still on. Oat cookies from the Willow House kitchen shelf. He dislikes bananas ("too squishy").

**Animals.** From the Willow House quiet room basket, he always picks the **one-eyed stuffed frog** first because "he's the most banged up," followed by the stuffed **owl** and **lamb**.

**Friends.**
- **Samir**, his best friend in second grade, who trades Pokémon cards with him on the bus.
- **Nora**, who sits next to him and gets annoyed when he tells her not to put her head down on her desk during quiet reading.

**What he wants to be.** An **emergency room doctor** or a **night watchman at the zoo** ("because somebody has to make sure the hippos don't roll over on each other").

## 2. Where and how they live **(decision)**

**The place.** A twin house in Philadelphia, ten minutes from Willow House by bus, with a small front porch and a driveway shared with the neighbors. It is **early February**, cold and slushy, about ten weeks after Wren's November session.

**Family and work.**
- **Mom (Claire), 38**, is a **dental hygienist** at a family practice. On her feet all day, comes home at 5:15 in scrubs with her shoulders aching.
- **Dad (Marcus), 40**, teaches **high-school history** and coaches JV basketball in the winter. Warm, practical, home a little later during basketball season.
- **Pop (Arthur, Mom's father)** lived four blocks away in a ground-floor apartment. He was a retired city transit mechanic who picked Leo up from the bus stop every Wednesday, shared peppermint sticks, and fixed things with a coffee can of spare screws.

## 3. What really happened

**December.** Pop had a bad heart for several years. One Tuesday night in December, he went to bed in his apartment and died peacefully in his sleep.

The next afternoon, Claire and Marcus sat Leo down on the living room rug. They wanted to explain it gently without scaring him with medical words about heart failure. So Claire held his hand and said: *"Pop was very old, Leo, and his body got very, very tired. So last night he went to sleep, and his body stopped working, and he didn't wake up."*

The day before Pop died, Leo had been at Pop's apartment after school. Pop had sat down in his armchair with the newspaper, closed his eyes for a minute, and said what he always said: *"I'm just resting my eyes, champ."*

**Since December.** Claire is grieving her father and is exhausted after work. When she comes home at 5:15, she lies down on the living room sofa for fifteen minutes before making dinner and says, *"I'm dead tired, honey, I'm just resting my eyes."* And when Marcus gets up from the floor after playing Legos, his knees pop and he groans, *"Man, I'm getting old."*

## 4. What Leo believes (the one true thing)

There is **no guilt** in Leo's story. He does not think he caused Pop's death. Instead, he has put together a terrifyingly logical seven-year-old rule:

- **Old + very tired + closing your eyes = how people die.**
- **Mom and Dad are starting to turn old.** Mom has **three white hairs** right above her left ear (he counted them). Dad has **wrinkles at the corners of his eyes** when he comes home from work, and his knees crack when he stands up. Pop had white hair and wrinkles around his eyes.
- **"I'm just resting my eyes" is the danger sign.** That is what Pop said right before he went to sleep and didn't wake up.
- **His job is to keep everyone checked and awake.** If he checks everyone's heartbeat, watches for white hairs and wrinkles, wakes Mom up whenever she closes her eyes on the couch, and checks from the hallway at night that his parents' blanket is still moving up and down, then nobody else in his house will stop breathing in the night.

## 5. What each person knows about him

- **Mom (Claire):** Thinks Leo is "wired" and regressing since Pop died—coming into their bedroom three times a night with random questions, and refusing to let her nap on the couch for even ten minutes.
- **Dad (Marcus):** Thinks Leo is trying to be the boss of the house because everyone has been sad, and notes that Leo has become obsessed with playing doctor.
- **Ms. Chen (second-grade teacher):** Wrote the school referral because Leo asked for a pass to the school nurse four times in two weeks with no fever or stomachache—he just wanted the nurse to listen to his chest with the stethoscope and tell him how many beats it made. Whenever Ms. Chen drinks coffee, he asks her if she is "too tired."
- **Isla:** Has the intake form, Ms. Chen's referral, Dr. Bell's margin note, and her own short note from Session One. She does not yet know about the three white hairs, the "no lying down" rule, or what Leo uses the red squeeze light for at night.

## 6. Isla and the sessions

This is **Leo's second session** (Tuesdays at 4:00):
1. **Session one (last week):** Leo spent the whole thirty minutes opening every drawer and bin in the playroom to see what was inside, found the toy doctor's kit in the last five minutes, and told Isla that next week they were opening a hospital.
2. **Session two (this episode):** Mom drops him off on time. When Isla greets him in the waiting room, he already has a plan: he wants the stuffed animals from upstairs brought down so he can run his clinic.

## 7. Leo's things and Willow House props

- **Pop's red squeeze light** (carried by Leo): A tiny red plastic flashlight on a key ring with the hardware store's name ("MILLER'S HARDWARE") half rubbed off. Pop got it free at the counter and clipped it to Leo's coat zipper. You have to keep squeezing the middle with your thumb for the little yellow beam to stay on; the moment you let go, it goes dark.
- **The toy doctor's kit** (on the playroom toy shelf): A plastic stethoscope, a wooden play syringe, a reflex hammer, and a roll of paper tape.
- **The stuffed animal patients** (from the 2nd-floor Quiet Room basket, or brought down by Isla): **Frog** (missing one button eye), **Owl**, and **Lamb**.
- **The clinic chart**: A sheet of blank drawing paper from the low table where Leo orders Nurse Isla to write down each patient's symptoms.

## 8. The secrets, as discoveries

Each is something Isla finds in the room through how Leo runs his clinic, held only when Isla stays inside his frame or answers plainly without probing:

1. **Secret 1 — The "No Lying Down" Rule.**
   Leo examines the one-eyed frog and the owl with the plastic stethoscope, then props all three animals bolt upright against the playroom baseboard. When Isla offers a cushion or bed, he says: *"Nobody lies down in my clinic. Lying down is how you get worse."*
2. **Secret 2 — Wrinkles and White Hair on the Chart.**
   Leo dictates the "Warning Signs" for Nurse Isla to write on the back of the paper chart: Owl has lines by his eyes when he comes home from work, and Frog has **three white hairs** right beside its ear (*"I counted them when she tied my shoes. Pop had a whole head of white hairs. That's how you get doctor tired, not regular tired."*). At pickup in the waiting room, Claire kneels to tie Leo's loose boot lace and tucks her hair behind her ear, where three white strands show against the dark.
3. **Secret 3 — "Just Resting My Eyes."**
   During the picture book, Leo stops and tells Isla confidentially that grown-ups trick you when they say *"I'm just resting my eyes"*—because Pop said that on the sofa the day before the ambulance came. So whenever Mom lies down on the sofa after work and closes her eyes, Leo drops his Lego bin or asks her spelling words until she opens them again.
4. **Secret 4 — The Red Squeeze Light (The Night Watch).**
   Leo unclips the tiny red hardware-store squeeze light from his jacket zipper to check the stuffed frog's good eye. If Isla holds the moment without probing or lecturing, he shows her how you have to keep squeezing it with your thumb so it doesn't go out—and tells her that after everyone goes to bed, he stands in the dark hallway outside his parents' door and squeezes it for two seconds just to see their blanket go up and down.

## 9. How he talks and bids (and the Fit Table)

Rules for every line Leo says:
- Brisk, bossy, concrete. Seven-to-twelve-word sentences.
- Calls Isla **"Nurse"** whenever they are in the clinic game, and **"Isla"** only when he drops the game for a second to say something real.
- Uses medical words slightly wrong (*"check his vitals," "he has the flops," "twenty-four beats"*).
- When a moment gets too close, he does not go quiet like Wren—he **makes a loud joke or invents a sudden medical emergency for the frog**.

**Fit table for Leo:**

| Leo's Bid | What Fits (+1 / +2) | What Hurts (0) |
|---|---|---|
| **Roleplay Order** (*"Nurse, write down twenty-two beats."*) | **Play Along / Join** (answer as Nurse, write it down, hand him the instrument) | **Break character / Probe** (*"Why are we checking so many hearts today?"*) |
| **Literal Question** (*"Does everybody with white hair get tired like that?"*) | **Plain, literal fact** (*"White hair just means someone is growing older. And regular tired goes away when you sleep."*) | **Euphemism or Vague Reassurance** (*"Your mom and dad are fine, don't worry."*) |
| **Step Back (Loud Joke)** (*"Beep beep! Frog swallowed a racecar!"*) | **Follow the joke for one beat, or Wait** | **Push him back to the serious topic** |
| **Tell (Secrets 1–4)** | **Reflect plainly (in or beside the nurse role) or Wait** | **Probe (*"Do you do that every night?"*) or Quick Fix (*"You don't need to check on your parents"*—he shuts down because he thinks someone has to!)** |

## 10. The three books

On the 3rd-floor Book Room shelves:
1. **Fits — *The Little Owl Who Kept Watch*:** A young owl tries to stay awake all day and all night marching up and down a branch with an acorn helmet so none of the other birds fall out of the tree while they sleep. His eyes get heavy and his feet ache, until the old badger explains the difference between the old hollow branch that broke in the winter storm and ordinary every-night sleeping, and shows Little Owl how every bird's chest goes up and down all by itself until morning.
2. **Shallow — *Doctor Rabbit's Busy Clinic*:** A cheerful book about band-aids, sneezes, and lollipops. Leo enjoys bossing Isla through the pages and reveals Secrets 1 and 2, but the book never touches sleeping or keeping watch, so Secrets 3 and 4 do not open.
3. **Trap (Too Direct & Uses the Euphemism) — *The Long Sleep in the Clouds*:** A gentle bereavement book on the `DEATH` shelf that opens with an old bear getting very tired and going to sleep on a cloud forever. The moment Isla reads page one, Leo says, *"That book is wrong. Sleeping isn't the same thing,"* shuts the cover, and goes back to the doctor kit.
