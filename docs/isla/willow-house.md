# Willow House: the place (draft)

The building and the rooms as they are for **any** child who comes. Nothing here depends on one family. What a particular child notices or does in the space belongs in that child's own document (for Wren, `wren-bible.md`). Items marked **(decision)** can change.

## 1. The building **(decision)**

A **1920s brick twin** (a house that shares one wall with its neighbor), three floors, on a corner lot in a Philadelphia rowhouse neighborhood. It was a doctor's house for sixty years, then empty, then bought with a foundation grant and a lot of donated labor. Willow House is a nonprofit children's counseling center with sliding-scale fees.

- **A deep front porch** with a swing that no clinician is allowed to remove. The door is painted a deep **sage green**, a shade darker than the playroom walls, with white trim, so the house and its rooms look like one thing.
- **An old weeping willow** in the front yard, a little too big for it. Its roots lift the sidewalk, and children hide under it while waiting for a parent. It gives the place its name.
- **A ramp** at the side door and a ground-floor playroom, so no child has to climb stairs.
- **A small fenced back yard** with a painted hopscotch, two herb beds the children water, and **back steps** that the staff use as a quiet place.
- Thick brick walls and old storm windows. Inside it is **quiet**, even though the street is not.

## 2. The street and the traffic **(decision)**

Willow House sits on the corner of **Linden Street**, a quiet one-way street with parked cars, and **Calloway Avenue**, a busy two-lane road with a **bus line**.

- **Sounds that come through the windows:** a bus sighing at the Calloway stop, a delivery truck backing up, the bell of a church down the block at the hour. With no clock in the room, they are how time passes.
- **Rush hour** is 3 to 6 p.m. School lets out at 3, and the street fills with children, strollers, and double-parked cars with their hazard lights on.
- **Parking is hard,** so parents double-park for drop-off or walk the last block from the bus.
- **Nearby:** a library branch, a laundromat, a corner store, a pharmacy, and a hospital six blocks away. A crossing guard works the corner at school hours.

## 3. The rooms **(decision)**

| Floor | Room | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Ground | **Entry and waiting room** | A small room with low chairs, a shelf of picture books, a fish tank, and a basket of fidgets. A reception desk on one side. |
| Ground | **The playroom** | The main room for play and reading. See section 5. |
| Ground | **Accessible bathroom** | Next to the playroom, with a step stool, a night light, and a lock that staff can open from outside. |
| Ground | **Kitchen** | At the back. A kettle, a fridge, and a **snack shelf**: crackers, clementines in winter, a water pitcher. Children can ask for a snack, and parents can make tea. |
| Second | **Two more therapy rooms** | A **sand and art room**, and a **quiet room** with a mattress and weighted blankets. |
| Second | **The family room** | Where parents meet a clinician. Two sofas, tissues, and a window onto the yard. |
| Third | **Offices** | The director's office and a shared staff room. |
| Third | **Storage and the book room** | Art supplies, spare toys, and the clinic's own small library of picture books. |
| Basement | **Laundry and storage** | Blankets and beanbag covers are washed constantly. |

**Nine rooms in all.** A child usually sees three: the waiting room, the playroom, and the bathroom.

## 4. What it is like inside **(decision)**

- **Smell:** old wood, crayons, warm laundry, and toast from the kitchen. Nothing strongly scented.
- **Sound:** radiators that knock and sigh in cold weather, a low white-noise machine in the hall, the fish tank filter, the floor creaking in two places.
- **Light:** soft and low. Afternoon sun comes through the front windows at an angle and crosses the floor.
- **Feel:** worn and warm, not clinical. Scuffed floors, mismatched chairs, a hallway wall of children's drawings with **no names on them**.
- **Signs:** hand-lettered and friendly. There is a rule on the playroom door, and nothing says "patient".

## 5. The playroom **(decision)**

**Size and shape.** About twelve by fifteen feet, a former front parlor with a bay window and original pine floors, worn in two paths where people have walked for a century.

**Colors.** Quiet and nobody's favorite:
- Walls a soft **sage green**. Trim white. A pale green blind on the window, so the afternoon light comes through gently.
- Warm natural **wood** furniture. Textiles in oatmeal, mustard, and dusty blue.
- No red, no bright primary colors, nothing that shouts.

**Furniture.** Everything at a child's height.
- A **low round table**, with chairs a child can climb onto alone.
- A **beanbag** by the window, deep and worn. It is the reading spot.
- A **low shelf of picture books**, with covers facing out, mostly bought by the staff themselves.
- **Two identical lidded toy bins** at either end of the shelf, for sorting toys back after play.
- A **rug printed with a road** and a little town along it. It is a game board, and some squares are more worn than others.
- A **soft chair** for the psychologist, **lower than average**, so her eyes are at a child's level.
- A **basket of crayons**, with one red crayon worn to a stub.
- A **wind-up timer** on the shelf, silent until the chime.

**Toys.** Open-ended and plain. Blocks, a small wooden train, a set of animal figures, a family of plain wooden dolls, hand puppets, a toy kitchen and a few cups, a doctor's kit, a box of costumes, a sand tray. Nothing battery-powered, nothing that tells the child what to do.

**Rules, on a card by the door** (read aloud the first time): *Nothing gets hurt. You can say it or not say it. When the timer chimes, we're done.*

## 6. How it makes children feel safe **(decision)**

| Feature | Why |
|---|---|
| The room is the same every week, and nothing moves | Predictability lets a child stop scanning |
| Child-height furniture, and the psychologist's chair is low | No one looms |
| Soft light, no fluorescent, a blind over the window | Calm, less glare |
| No mirror, no camera, and no notes taken in the room | Nobody is watching or recording a child |
| The door has a low handle and can stay open | A child can leave, or look for a parent |
| The family room and waiting room are close | A parent is always near |
| The rules card, and a **timer** that ends the session | Limits are clear, and ending is the timer's job |
| Comfort objects are welcome | A child brings their own safety |
| A white-noise machine in the hall | Nobody outside can hear |
| Choice offered, never forced: where to sit, whether to talk | Control is how fear eases |
| A snack shelf | Being fed is a way of being cared for |
| Rounded edges, nothing sharp or breakable | Nothing can go wrong in a way that matters |
| **A pickup card** on the wall of each therapy room, showing a photo of whoever is collecting the child and the time | No child is ever unsure who is coming for them, or when |

## 7. How it keeps a child focused on the story **(decision)**

- The **reading nook** is a separate zone: the beanbag, a lamp with a warm bulb, and a low shelf close by. Reading happens there, not at the table.
- **Before the story,** Isla draws a **cloth** over the toy shelf and says: "Stories first. Toys are sleeping." It is her own habit, and she keeps the cloth in her tote. The toys stop competing, and a child is welcome to help her pull it over.
- The **lamp** goes on and the **overhead** light goes off. The room gets smaller and warmer.
- The **door** is closed to a hand's width. A small **paper bird** hangs on the knob outside: a quiet signal for staff.
- **The child holds the book,** or turns the pages, if they want. The adult reads aloud, and the child can stop her at any time.
- The **timer** is wound but not visible, so no one watches the clock.
- The same page is **read again** if asked, and no one rushes.
- No phones, no screens, no clocks on the wall.

## 8. How a session runs **(decision)**

1. A parent or carer signs in at the front desk. The receptionist checks the **pickup card**: who is collecting, and when.
2. The child waits in the waiting room, or under the willow, until the psychologist comes to the door.
3. They walk to the playroom together. The rules card is read the first time.
4. The session lasts **thirty minutes**: some play, a book, and a few minutes at the end to tidy up.
5. The timer gives a **five-minute** soft warning, and then the chime.
6. The parent collects the child. A parent meeting with the family therapist happens separately, usually in the family room.
