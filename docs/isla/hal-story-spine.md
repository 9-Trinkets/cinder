# Hal: story spine for the first act (draft)

Companion to `session-design.md`. Fully authored, no LLM. Hal is a new patient written for the bid-and-secret design; he replaces Noa for the first build (`noa-story-spine.md` is kept for reference). Not playtested.

## 1. Who he is

**Hal**, 71, a retired land surveyor. His wife **Ruth** died in March, quickly. His daughter **Dana** made the appointment, and he came to please her. He is dry, formal, funny, and says he is "managing perfectly well." Surveyors spend forty years on where lines are, so he is precise about everything except the one line he cannot look at.

Tone: dry humor first, so the reveals land harder. Everyday intimacy, no graphic detail.

## 2. The shape of his story

A man who is following his late wife's instructions to the letter, because following them means she is still there.

Each secret moves one step closer to him:

| # | Secret | What it sounds like | Closer to him |
|---|--------|---------------------|---------------|
| S1 | Two cups | a quirk | a small ritual that is really presence |
| S2 | The closet | tidiness | he refuses to be the one who moves the first thing |
| S3 | The list | efficiency | he follows her written instructions so she is still telling him what to do |
| S4 | The anger | a confession | he hasn't cried, he is furious she went first, and putting the list down feels like losing her |

The theme is radical acceptance: facing that she is gone without having to approve of it.

## 3. The book

***The Bear Who Followed the Map*** (twelve spreads). A heron leaves a bear a map before leaving. The bear follows every step.

| Spread | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | The heron leaves a map folded into the bear's paw: "For when I am not here." |
| 2 | Step one: fish at dawn. Two bowls are on the table; one is eaten. |
| 3 | Step two: beans planted exactly in rows. The bear is proud; a rabbit waves from the fence. |
| 4 | The rabbit brings jam. "Can't stop, I'm following the map." The jam stays on the step. |
| 5 | Step seven: the bridge is out. The bear sits at the edge and folds the map smaller. |
| 6 | The bear goes home the long way. The heron's side of the nest is untouched. |
| 7 | The last step on the map is blank, with a smudge where the pen rested. |
| 8 | The bear tries to write the last step himself, and crosses it out, twice. |
| 9 | Rain. The bear sits with the map. "The map doesn't know about the rain." |
| 10 | The rabbit returns with the jam: "Come in out of the rain." The map says nothing about this. |
| 11 | The bear says, small and cross, "You didn't say what to do." No one answers. |
| 12 | The bear goes in. The map stays in his pocket. He eats the jam. |

Stops are after spreads **3, 5, 7, 9 and 12**.

## 4. Plants and payoffs (clues that do not score)

| Plant (early) | Pays off in |
|---|---|
| His watch runs seven minutes fast: "Ruth set it so I'd be on time." | S3: he still follows her system |
| He takes out a notepad and pen "to make notes" | S3: he is a man of lists |
| He says "we usually... I usually" and corrects himself | S1, S2 |
| Tea only "brewed properly, with the pot warmed first" | S1, S3: her way of doing things |
| He puts his hat on the chair beside him, then moves it | S1 |
| "Forty years of surveys. Most disputes are about where someone thinks the line is." | S2, S4 |

## 5. The act, bid by bid

Ordinary bids are 1 point each (first try only). Secrets are 2 points each and score only if held. Total available: 8 + 4x2 = **16 points**.

### Ordinary bids

| # | When | Type | His line | Why it matters |
|---|------|------|----------|----------------|
| 1 | Arrival | Invitation | "Where do I sit? There's usually a protocol." | he wants rules |
| 2 | Intake | Feeling | "I'm managing perfectly well. Ask anyone. Ask Dana." | protests too much |
| 3 | Intake | Feeling | "Forty years of surveys. Most disputes are about where someone thinks the line is." | plant for S2 and S4 |
| 4 | Serve | Deflection | "No tea for me. Well, only if it's brewed properly. Pot warmed first." | plant for S1 and S3 |
| 5 | Stop 1 (after spread 3) | Feeling | "Sensible bear. Follows instructions. Nothing wrong with that." | the map echo begins |
| 6 | Stop 1 | Invitation | "Go on. What's the next step?" | he wants to continue |
| 7 | Stop 2 (after spread 5) | Invitation | "Why doesn't the bear ask the rabbit about the bridge?" | he asks what he cannot ask himself |
| 8 | Stop 4 (after spread 9) | Feeling | "A long time to sit in the rain with a piece of paper." | opens the way to S3 |

### Secrets

Each is an Opening bid. **Held** means Reflect his words or Wait. Anything else is **fumbled**: he gives a deflecting line, the fact is known, and nothing scores.

**S1: Two cups.** After spread 5 (the bridge is out and the bear sits).
- *Telling:* "I put out two cups every morning. Habit. [a beat] I know she isn't coming down."
- *Held:* "I wash both. I don't know why that part matters." Reframe: a small ritual of presence, not foolishness.
- *Fumbled:* "Old habits. Anyway, the bridge."

**S2: The closet.** After spread 7 (the last step is blank).
- *Telling:* "Her side of the closet is exactly as it was. I sleep on my side. Dana wants to help me sort things. I tell her I've sorted them."
- *Held:* "I can't be the one to move the first thing. Then I'd have done it." Reframe: he is not keeping clutter; he is refusing to end it.
- *Fumbled:* "It's tidy. I'm a tidy man."

**S3: The list.** After spread 9 (rain, the bear and the map).
- *Telling:* "She left a list. Written in February, in the kitchen drawer. 'Water the ferns on Tuesdays. Call Dana on Sundays. Don't let the car insurance lapse.' Forty-one items. I've done every one."
- *Held:* "As long as I'm doing the list, she's still telling me what to do. [a pause] I'd rather have her telling me what to do." Reframe: the list is a way to keep her.
- *Fumbled:* "It's a very good list. Practical woman."

**S4: The anger.** After spread 12. Only if S3 was held.
- *Telling:* "I haven't cried. Not once. [a beat] I'm angry with her, if you want to know. She went first. She had no business going first."
- *Held:* a short, surprised laugh. "Don't tell Dana." Then: "Maybe I'll skip a Tuesday. See what happens." (Keep it this modest. No neat resolution.)
- *Fumbled:* "Sorry. That's more than you bargained for." He checks his watch.

### The gate

He tells a secret only if you have earned at least **60% of the points available so far** when its moment arrives. S4 also needs S3 held. A hurt counts as unmet and he becomes more guarded. Thresholds are guesses.

## 6. Closing and review

**Closing menu** offers only lines he actually said, so it depends on how far you got. Up to four of:
- "I'm managing perfectly well." (surface)
- "I wash both. I don't know why that part matters." (S1 held)
- "I can't be the one to move the first thing." (S2 held)
- "I'd rather have her telling me what to do." (S3 held)
- "Maybe I'll skip a Tuesday." (S4 held)

The **real insight is the deepest held line available**. If he never reached S3, the closing can only be a lesser one.

**Review.** Written by Hal at home, dry and formal. Facts that pick authored lines: S1 held, S2 held, S3 held, S4 held, book fit, closing line chosen. A held secret earns a warm line, an unheld one earns silence. Examples of tone:
- Low: "Three stars. The tea was acceptable. The bear book is not about a bear."
- High: "Five stars. I will be skipping a Tuesday. Do not tell Dana."

## 7. Open questions

1. **Does the book mirror the story too directly?** The map is the list. That is the point, but it may be too on the nose. Alternative: a book where the match is less literal.
2. **Near-miss book.** Proposal: a book about staying busy (*The Very Busy Beaver*) that reaches only S1 and S2.
3. **Number of stops.** Five (after spreads 3, 5, 7, 9, 12). The design said three or four. Merge bid 8 into stop 3 or 4 if it feels long.
4. **Is S4 reachable in one act?** It needs S3 held and a high score. That may suit a first act and replay, or feel out of reach.
5. **Gate threshold.** 60% is a guess. Test in the prototype.
6. **Content care.** Keep it at the level of two cups and a list, never graphic.
