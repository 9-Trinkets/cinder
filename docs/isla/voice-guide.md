# Isla: voice guide (draft)

How everyone in the Wren and Tam acts sounds, and how the story is told. Read with `wren-bible.md` and `tam-bible.md`. Sections 1 to 19 are Wren's act and the shared rules. Sections 20 to 29 are Tam's cast and what changes when a child says almost nothing. The example lines are **calibration only**, not final script. Every in-game line follows the conventions in section 1.

## 1. Conventions for every line

- **Speech** is written as its own line, naming who it is said to: `Wren (to Isla): what she says.` No stage directions inside the speech. Action goes in a separate narration line before or after.
- **Narration** is its own line. Two sentences at most.
- **No parentheses** in anything the player reads, except the `(to Name)` label on speech.
- **American English**: Mom, Dad, first grade, recess, sidewalk, cookie.
- **A child who barely speaks gets almost no speech lines.** In Tam's act most turns are narration of what he does (section 21). Speech is for the adults, and for the one or two words he says.
- **Nothing is explained.** The text never says what a behavior means ("she is hiding her sadness"). It shows the behavior and lets the player decide.
- **Isla's options** are things Isla says or does, written in Isla's voice (section 3). They are never labeled good or bad.
- **Book text** is read aloud by Isla, so it is her speech: `Isla (to Wren): ...`, always in the picture-book voice (section 14). It follows a line of narration that describes the picture.

## 2. The narrative voice

**Second person, present tense, plain.** It speaks to Isla and tells only what Isla can see and hear.

**Does:**
- Describe behavior and objects exactly: "Wren sets the red crayon at the end of the row."
- Use sound and small physical detail: the click of the bin lid, the hum.
- Let pauses be pauses: "She doesn't answer."
- Keep paragraphs short, and keep the camera low, at a child's height.

**Does not:**
- Read minds or name feelings. Never "Wren looks sad" or "she feels guilty".
- Interpret. Never "clearly this matters to her".
- Use clinical language: no "affect", "presenting", "regress", "masking".
- Use the adult's retrospective voice ("later you would remember...").
- Sentimentalize. The tone is warm and dry, not wistful.

**Calibration:**
> You are Isla. You read picture books with children, and you notice what they do with them.
> Wren walks in holding a stuffed rabbit by one ear. She goes straight to the two bins and starts moving toys from one to the other.

**Not this:** "Wren trudges in, clearly carrying the weight of her parents' divorce."

## 3. Isla (the player's voice)

Calm, curious, plain. A grown-up who is in no hurry and wants nothing from the child.

**Rules for every option Isla can say:**
- **Short.** One sentence, usually five to ten words.
- **Comments over questions.** "You're putting the dog in this one." beats "Why are you putting the dog there?"
- **At most one question at a time**, and never a "why" or "how do you feel".
- **Picks up Wren's meaning without parroting her last three words.** Reflecting is not mechanical repetition ("In case." / "The whole time."). Answer like a warm, observant adult who heard the whole thought: "Usually Mom brings you on Thursdays." "That way Biscuit has everything he needs, no matter which house you're at."
- **Wonders** instead of asking: "I wonder what Rabbit thinks."
- **Never praises or judges** ("good job", "that's wrong").
- **Never reassures too early.** No "it's okay", no "it wasn't your fault" until it is earned.
- **Child-level words.** No abstract nouns: no "situation", "family dynamic", "transition".
- **Silence and following are real options.** Isla can sit, watch, or hand over a crayon.

**Calibration (Isla):**
- "Those ones live here."
- "Rabbit's coming too."
- "You're lining them up by color."
- "I wonder what Biscuit thinks about that."
- "You can stop whenever you like."

**Not these:** "I can see you're upset." "Why do you think you're doing that?" "It's okay to feel sad."

## 4. Wren (six and a half)

Full rules are in the bible, section 13. In short: four to eight words, "and then", concrete nouns, no feeling words, answers feelings with action, changes the subject when it is too big, tells true things through Rabbit.

**Extra rules:**
- She talks about Rabbit in the third person. When she wants something hard said, she whispers it: "Rabbit says..."
- She asks for **"same"** and **"fair"**, and says "not fair" when something is uneven.
- She reports in the present tense: "I'm putting him in. He's in."
- She answers a question with the smallest true answer. "Do you like it?" gets "It's okay."
- She counts out loud when nervous, then stops herself.
- A bright, irrelevant fact is her exit: "I can do a cartwheel." "Pretzel's tail is tiny."
- She is not precocious. She does not explain herself, and she is sometimes silly.

**Calibration (Wren):**
- "These ones live here."
- "Rabbit doesn't live in a box. He lives in my bag."
- "It's Tuesday. Tuesday is the green door."
- "Don't tell. He won't tell either."
- "It came off all in one bit."
- "I can do a cartwheel. Not in here."

## 5. Mom (Nadia, 34)

Warm, brisk, tired. A classroom aide, so her default voice is calm and organized, even when she is not.

- Full sentences, lists, and "okay" as a full stop.
- Says **"it's fine"** when it is not.
- Calls Wren "baby", and uses the name "Wren" when she is worried.
- Apologizes a little too often.
- When she is hurt she gets quieter and more precise, not louder.
- Her written note is short, in contractions, with no drama.

**Calibration (Mom):**
- "Okay, baby. Half past four. I'll be right here."
- "She's been so good. Honestly, she's no trouble at all."
- "I'm sorry. It's been a long few months."

## 6. Dad (Ben, 36)

Gentle, a bit scattered, quick with a joke when he feels guilty. A carpenter, so his comparisons come from the workshop.

- Calls Wren "kiddo".
- Starts sentences and does not finish them: "I just want her to..."
- Uses carpentry sayings: "measure twice", "it'll hold".
- Over-offers: food, treats, plans.
- Quiet pride in things he has built.

**Calibration (Dad):**
- "Hey, kiddo. Have fun. I'll, uh, see you Tuesday."
- "I built her a bunk bed. She's got the whole top. Well. She picked the bottom."
- "I just want her to be okay. You know?"

## 7. Aunt Jo (Dad's sister)

Blunt, easy, funny. Asks no questions. Treats Wren like a small, capable person.

- Says things plainly, without softening.
- Talks to the dog as much as to people.
- Never says "how are you feeling".

**Calibration (Aunt Jo):**
- "Hey, you. Mustard missed you. He sat on my foot all morning."
- "You don't have to say anything. Hold the leash."

## 8. Nana (Mom's mother, 71)

Soft, slow, old-fashioned, easily tired.

- Endearments: "sweet pea", "my darling".
- Short breaths between sentences.
- Never complains. Says she is "just resting".

**Calibration (Nana):**
- "There's my sweet pea. Come sit. I'm just resting."

## 9. Ms. Okafor (first-grade teacher)

Kind, professional, specific. Describes behavior and never gives a diagnosis.

- Concrete observations: "She sorts the library books at lunch."
- "I noticed" instead of "she is".
- Gentle and a little worried.

**Calibration (Ms. Okafor):**
- "She used to share news every morning. Lately she just says pass."

## 10. Mr. Dee (the crossing guard)

Cheerful, short, formulaic. Says the same thing every morning.

**Calibration (Mr. Dee):**
- "Eyes up, feet moving. Look both ways, then look again."

## 11. Marisol (best friend, six)

Bossy, funny, confused. Says what she thinks right away.

**Calibration (Marisol):**
- "You never play tag anymore. You're a statue."

## 12. Teddy (classmate, six)

Quiet. One sentence at a time, usually about Pretzel.

**Calibration (Teddy):**
- "Pretzel stuffed all of it in his cheeks."

## 13. Rabbit (Biscuit)

Does not speak. Wren speaks for him in a whisper, always shorter than she speaks, and always about the hard thing.

**Calibration:** `Wren: Rabbit says he doesn't mind.`

## 14. The book voice

Picture-book voice: third person, past tense, short declaratives, repetition, a gentle rhythm, a little humor, no moral stated.

- Characters have simple names: Bird, Rabbit, Owl, the Two Trees.
- Sentences repeat a pattern the child can join in on: "So Bird flew on."
- Nothing is explained at the end. The last page shows, and does not tell.
- A plain sentence after each page describes the picture.

**Calibration:**
> Isla (to Wren): Bird had a nest. It was a good nest. She carried it from the first tree to the second tree, and from the second tree back to the first. And she never put it down.

## 15. Gloria (office manager, nineteen years)

Warm, unhurried, practical. She runs the front desk and knows every child's name, every parent's car and which child likes which sticker. She talks the way someone talks while doing something else: a little small talk, a task, a kind word.

- Complete, casual sentences. "Hon" and "sweetheart" for anyone, and first names for parents.
- **Reports behavior as chores and small facts.** "She's been counting the stones in the tank." Never as a diagnosis, and never an opinion about a family.
- Answers the question she was asked and stops. She does not summarize events.
- Dry and fond. Her jokes are about the building, the snail, the printer.
- She does not give instructions or hints. If she nudges, it is by offering something: a sticker, a cup of water.

**Calibration (Gloria):**
- "Go on in, hon. She's fine."
- "She asked me what day it is. Twice. I said Thursday both times."
- "That printer has opinions today."

**Not these:** "Her dad dropped her at ten to and was gone before I'd put my pen down. Job across town." (a recap, a writer's flourish, a fragment). "You'll want to read up before you take her." (a hint wearing her name).

## 16. Dr. Bell (director)

Sixty, warm, brief. She sees everything and says very little of it, and never in a hurry. When she writes, it is a sentence or two.

- Whole sentences, no abbreviations or texting style.
- One question at most, and never a leading one.
- Treats Isla as a colleague, not a student.

**Calibration (Dr. Bell):**
- "How did it go? Come and find me tomorrow."
- "Take your time on the steps."

## 17. Isla with adults

Dry, brief, polite. With adults Isla answers with less than she was asked and does not explain. She asks plain questions, because adults can take them.

**Calibration (Isla):**
- "How's she doing?"
- "She's early."
- "Thanks, Gloria."

## 18. How robotic reads, and how to fix it

Check every line against these:
- **Is it information or is it speech?** If a line exists so the player learns a fact, rewrite it as what that person would notice or ask for.
- **Does it summarize?** People answer the question and stop. They do not recap events with a time and a reason.
- **Is it a fragment for effect?** "Job across town." Real fragments answer something just said.
- **Is it a flourish?** If the line has a clever image, would the person say it out loud at their desk?
- **Clock times and British phrasing.** No "ten to four", no "I'd put". American: "about ten minutes early".
- **Say it aloud.** If you would not say it, neither would they.

## 19. Check before showing anything

Read each path as plain transcript text and ask:
1. Could a six-year-old plausibly say this line?
2. Is anyone naming a feeling they should be showing?
3. Is it clear who is speaking, and when time has passed?
4. Is anything explained that the player should discover?
5. Are there any parentheses, clinical words, or British spellings?

## 20. Tam's act: what changes

Wren answers, so her act is mostly speech. Tam does not, so his act is mostly **things Isla does and things he does.** Three rules follow.

- **Isla speaks less, and never to ask Tam anything.** One short sentence at most, a comment about the room or the thing in front of them, said once. Often she says nothing and the option is an action: lower the blind, turn the machine down, sit on the floor.
- **No question is ever the right move,** so the options list never offers one. A line that ends in a question mark is spoken only to an adult.
- **Waiting is written, not skipped.** A beat where nothing is asked has a line of narration: a sound, a small movement, the light. It is what makes the pause readable.

## 21. Tam (six)

Almost no words. What he does is the speech.

**What he can say:** *again*, *train*, *up*, *no*, *Ông*, and, once, *Mai*. Never a sentence. Never a question. Said low, to an object or to the air more often than to a person.

**How his words are written.** `Tam (to Isla): again.` Lowercase, one word, a full stop. Even when he is looking at a book, the label names the person nearest him, so the speech parses like every other line. A narration line before it says where he is looking: "He turns back one page and puts his finger on the bridge."

**How his behavior is written.** Plain, exact, present tense, at a child's height.
- Objects and position: "He lines the animals up on the rug, shortest to longest."
- Small repeated motions: "He taps twice on the table. Then he sits."
- Sound: "He hums, low and even, da-dum, da-dum. The radiator knocks, and the hum stops."
- A body, never a feeling: "He covers one ear with the heel of his hand." Not "the sound hurts him".
- **No inner life.** Never "he wants", "he seems", "he is afraid", "he is relieved". If the player should know it, something he does shows it.
- **Not sad, not absent, not special.** He is busy, exact, a little bossy about his trains. A line may be funny.

**Calibration (Tam):**
- Narration: "He stops at the door. He looks at the ceiling light, then at the window."
- Narration: "He takes a small orange car out of his pocket and sets it down, wheels on the floor."
- `Tam (to Isla): again.`

**Not these:** "Tam looks overwhelmed." "He seems to relax." "He says nothing, but his eyes say it all."

## 22. Ông (Tam's grandfather, sixty-eight)

Dignified, careful, brief. He came to Philadelphia as an adult and speaks careful English that he does not rush. He is the person who understands Tam best and he does not say so.

- Short, complete, correct sentences. Few contractions. Occasionally a plain word order that is a little formal.
- **Calls Isla "Cô,"** as a child would call a teacher. Isla calls him "Mr. Tran."
- Calls Tam **"bé Tam."**
- **Asks for nothing and offers little.** He answers the question and stops. He does not explain Tam, apologize for him or advise Isla.
- Watches more than he talks. A line of narration about what he does with his hands (the folded newspaper, the hat) is worth three of speech.
- **Vietnamese stays out of the transcript** except *Ông*, *Cô* and *bé Tam*. When he speaks to Tam in Vietnamese the narration says so and gives no words: "He says something to Tam in Vietnamese, low and quick."
- His English is a person's English, not a type's. No dropped articles for flavor.

**Calibration (Ông):**
- "Good afternoon, Cô. He is ready, I think."
- "He likes to see the door first."
- "Thank you, Cô. We will come again Tuesday."

**Not these:** "He is a good boy, very quiet, very smart, you must understand our culture." (explains him and the family). Any pidgin.

## 23. Linh (Tam's mother, thirty-three, night-shift nurse)

Exact, tired, careful in both languages. A nurse, so her default voice is calm and factual even when she is not calm. She is quick to hear a clinician managing her.

- Full sentences, specifics, times and amounts. Facts first, feeling never.
- **Says "he is fine" when she is checking if he is.**
- Asks one precise question where another parent would ask five vague ones.
- She is wary of labels, and of the word "responding." She does not argue. She goes very polite.
- She never says she is exhausted. She mentions that she slept two hours.
- Her message is short, written on a break, with no greeting beyond a name.

**Calibration (Linh):**
- "Thank you for seeing him. I slept this morning, so I'm able to read this properly."
- "He ate the whole rice. He went to the window first. I thought you would want to know."
- "Dũng will call the school. Would a quieter corner be something you can put in writing?"

## 24. Dũng (Tam's father, thirty-five, pharmacist)

Clear, courteous, fluent in institutions. He talks to people all day and has little left at home. He is the one who will take Isla's note to the school.

- Organized speech: "two things", "first", "the short version".
- Comfortable with forms, dates and phone calls, and gently insistent on the right wording.
- Dry about his own tiredness. A pharmacist's habit of checking that he has understood: "So that's once a day, at night."
- Never describes Tam by what he lacks.

**Calibration (Dũng):**
- "Two things, if that's all right. First, what should I tell the school?"
- "He's just quiet. That's how he's always been. I'd like to be sure that's all it is."

## 25. The rest of the family

They are mostly offstage, named in notes and in what Tam carries. If any of them speaks, keep to one line each.
- **Mai (six months).** A sound, never a word. "A baby cries somewhere upstairs in the house you are thinking of." Only ever in notes, never in the room.
- **Bà Ngoại (Linh's mother, Houston).** Video calls on Sundays. Loving, insistent: "Eat. Sleep. He will come out of it."
- **Cô Thu (Dũng's sister).** Loud, loving. One line in a note at most.
- **Cam (the orange cat).** Does not speak. "Cam sleeps on his feet" is the whole of her.

## 26. Ms. Reyes (first-grade teacher) and Dev (classmate, six)

- **Ms. Reyes.** Kind, specific, a little defensive about the plan. "I noticed" and concrete observations: "He stopped coming to circle time in January." Never "withdrawn" in speech. The school's word appears only in the typed note, and the family's objection to it is written next to it.
- **Dev.** Six, like Wren's classmates. One sentence about trains, no explanation: "He knows all the cars."

## 27. Gloria and Dr. Bell with Tam

- **Gloria** does not coax. She puts out a sticker or a cup of water and says nothing about either. She says "Hi, Tam" once and does not repeat it if he does not look up.
- **Dr. Bell's margin** is two or three plain commands, no softeners: "Go slowly. Fewer words. Let him decide when he comes in."

## 28. Isla with Tam, and with his family

- **With Tam:** one sentence, in Isla's own plain voice, a comment about the room or the thing between them. Never a question, never praise. "The lamp's on." "The blind's down." "I'll read it again."
- **With Ông:** she is brief and warm, and plainly respectful. "Good afternoon, Mr. Tran. You can wait right here, or in the kitchen." She asks him nothing about Tam.
- **With Linh and Dũng:** the adult register of section 17, with a pharmacist and a nurse she does not talk down to. She says what she saw, once, and what she would try, and stops.

**Calibration (Isla with adults, this act):**
- "Good afternoon, Mr. Tran."
- "We'll start slowly. He doesn't need to come in until he's ready."

## 29. Check before showing anything from Tam's act

In addition to section 19:
1. **Does any line say what Tam feels, wants or understands?** Replace it with something he does.
2. **Does Isla ask Tam anything?** Remove it. A question belongs to an adult.
3. **Does Tam say more than one word?** Cut it. *Mai* is said once, and only at the top ending.
4. **Is there Vietnamese beyond *Ông*, *Cô* and *bé Tam*?** Take it out, or give it only in narration, with no words.
5. **Does the family explain itself or its culture?** Cut it. They are people, and nobody says why they are the way they are.
6. **Does a family member's English read as a type's?** Rewrite it as a person's.
7. **Has a native reader checked the names, titles and kin terms,** before anything goes into the shipped text?
