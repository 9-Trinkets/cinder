# Tam: story bible (draft, no scripted lines yet)

Everything the writing must stay true to for the second patient. Written the way `wren-bible.md` is: facts first, lines later. Read with `next-patient.md` (why this child and how his episode differs), `game-design.md` (the diner structure, fit tables and the transitions between episodes) and `isla-bible.md`. Items marked **(decision)** are choices I made that can change.

**Content stays everyday and non-graphic.** Nobody in this family is a villain, and nothing here is abuse or neglect. Tam is not a problem to solve. The game shows a child whose world is louder than he can carry, and an adult who notices.

**A note on care.** Tam is autistic. The game never says the word and never explains him. It shows what he does and treats it as a way of being a person. Isla's skill is learning to read him, and the fix is a practical kindness, not a cure. Danny's life belongs to Danny, and Tam is not Danny.

**A note on the family.** The Tran family is Vietnamese American, in South Philadelphia. Their background changes what the home smells and sounds like, who is in the room, what the school label means to them and what a clinic asks of them. It is never the reason for Tam's difficulty, and nothing in the text should explain a person by their culture. Isla is a white woman from small-town Pennsylvania, and she does not know everything about this house. The details here should be checked with a reader from the community before any lines are final.

## 1. Who he is

**Tam** (Tâm), six (birthday in September). First grade. Small and quick, with a cowlick that will not lie flat. He is gentle, exact and very sure of what he likes. At home his name is **Tam**, and his grandfather calls him *bé Tam*, "little Tam."

**What he can and cannot do** (the writing depends on this):
- **He speaks very little.** He has a handful of words, mostly at home and in two languages: *nữa* (more, again), *xe lửa* (train), *lên* (up), *không* (no), *Ông* (his grandfather), and in English *again* and *train* when he says them at school. He understands almost everything said to him, in both languages, and answers with his hands and his body.
- **He reads a room better than most adults.** He notices a change in the light, a new sound, who is tired.
- **He can do the same thing for a very long time,** and it is not boredom. It is how he settles.
- **He cannot filter noise.** Everything arrives at once, so a busy room costs him more than it costs anyone else.
- **He cannot be hurried,** and does not understand why a thing he has started should be interrupted.
- **He does not mind silence,** and is not waiting to be asked anything.

## 2. Tam as a person **(decision)**

**Temperament.** Careful, exact, a little solemn, and kind in a way that shows in what he does and not what he says. He shares by handing you something. When he is at ease he hums.

**Habits**
- **Lines things up by length,** shortest to longest, left to right. He moves a piece that is out of order, quietly, as soon as he sees it.
- **Checks the same few things** each time he enters a place: the door, the window, the light switch. He is not worried. It is how he learns a room.
- **Hums the sound of a train going over a bridge,** a low, even *da-dum, da-dum*. He does it when he is calm and stops when he is not.
- **Taps twice** on a surface before he sits.
- **Carries his backpack on both shoulders** and does not put it down. It holds the book (section 11).
- **Covers one ear** with the heel of his hand when a sound is too much, and does not seem to notice he is doing it.
- **Goes under a table** when everything is too much. It is not a tantrum. It is a place.

**What he is good at.** Trains: he knows every car in a freight train by its shape, and can tell a bridge by its sound. Putting things in order. Finding the one piece that is missing. Waiting, with enormous patience, when he is not hurried.

**What makes him laugh.** A train horn on the radio. A toy that falls over slowly. Ông doing the sound of a train badly on purpose.

**Colors.** Orange and silver, the colors of the cars he watches go by. He does not like bright white light.

**Food.** The same few things, in the same order, with nothing touching: plain rice in a bowl, steamed chicken in strips, a boiled egg, peeled slices of Asian pear. He likes the sound of the rice cooker clicking off. He does not like soup that is too hot to see into.

**Animals.** An old orange cat named **Cam** ("orange," in Vietnamese), who sleeps on his feet. Tam does not move until the cat does.

**Friends.** One, a first-grade boy named **Dev**, who also likes trains and does not need him to talk. They sit side by side and line up cars.

**What he wants to be.** He has never been asked. In a drawing at school he is a train conductor holding a flag.

**Favorite places.**
- **A rail embankment** a few blocks from home, with Ông, at the hour the freight goes by.
- **Under the dining table at home,** where the tablecloth makes a room.
- **At school:** the quiet corner, when he is allowed to go there.

**Favorite class.** Music, where he can listen to the drum without having to do anything.

## 3. Where and how they live **(decision)**

**The place.** A block of narrow brick rowhouses in **South Philadelphia**, a short walk from the Washington Avenue corridor with its Vietnamese groceries, bakeries and noodle shops. Neighbors know each other. Freight and passenger trains run on an embankment a few blocks away, and the windows hum when a heavy one passes.

**Money.** **Stretched, in a particular way:** both parents work and the household runs on them taking turns. Nobody is poor and nobody has any slack.
- **Má (Linh), 33,** is a **night-shift nurse** at a hospital. She sleeps in the daytime, and the house has to be quiet while she does. She was born in Philadelphia, speaks English at work and Vietnamese at home, and is more careful with each language than she lets anyone see.
- **Ba (Dũng), 35,** is a **bus mechanic** at a transit depot who works days. He came to the United States as a small child, and knows engines and rails from the inside.
- **Ông Nội (Ông), 68,** Dũng's father, a retired bus operator who came to Philadelphia as a refugee in the 1980s. He lives with them. He walks Tam to school and back, in his careful English and his own Vietnamese, and greets the shopkeepers on the way.
- A **baby sister, Mai** (named for the apricot blossom of the new year), six months old. She cries in the night.

**The house.** A two-bedroom rowhouse with a narrow stair. A small altar on a shelf in the front room holds photographs and a bowl of fruit. Tam's room used to be at the back, the quietest room. When Mai was born, the nursery was made out of the room next to it, and the wall between them is thin.

## 4. School **(decision)**

- Tam is in a **public school first-grade classroom** with a teacher, **Ms. Reyes**, and an aide in for part of the day.
- He has a plan that allows a **quiet corner**, but the corner is next to the reading rug, where the noise is worst, and the plan says he *may* go, not that he *should*.
- When he started kindergarten, the first worry was **language:** a child who is quiet in English, from a home where another language is spoken, is often assumed to be still learning. Linh and Dũng had to push to have him evaluated for something else, and it took a year. They are wary of the school's words, and they do not like the sound of "responding."
- He has stopped joining **circle time,** the one group activity he used to like. The school counselor suggested Willow House, and Linh agreed on one condition: that nobody describes him as less than he is.
- He is still a good student. He finishes his work quietly, ahead of everyone else, and then sits.

## 5. Extended family

- **Ông** is his best person. He asks nothing, hums back, and takes him to the embankment.
- **Bà Ngoại** (Linh's mother) lives in Houston and calls on Sundays by video. She wants Tam to say her name, and loves him fiercely. She believes he needs feeding and sleep and fresh air, and that he will "come out of it."
- **Cô Ba** (Dũng's sister) is a loud, loving, well-meaning aunt who pinches cheeks and tickles children whether they want it or not. She is not a villain, but Tam goes quiet when she visits.
- **Cousins** come for the new year, and the house fills.

## 6. What really happened **(decision)**

**Before.** Tam has always been a quiet child. At four, an evaluation (with an interpreter, which Linh did not need and Ông did) gave a word for it. His parents learned how to make a world he could live in: soft lights, a routine, headphones for the loudest places, a room of his own. It worked. He was happy.

**The baby (this spring).** Mai was born in March. The house changed in a way no one planned for: night crying, two adults who are very tired, a nursery on the other side of his wall, a month of visitors for the baby's celebration. His room, the one place built to be quiet, stopped being quiet. He stopped sleeping well, and by day he had nothing left over for school.

**Now (late winter).** It is the weeks before the **Lunar New Year,** when the house is cleaned, relatives come and go, and the street fills with firecrackers and lion-dance drums. Everything is louder than ever, and everyone is doing it out of love.

**What it looks like from outside.** To his teacher, he has withdrawn: he doesn't join circle time, he doesn't answer, he goes under the table. To Cô Ba, he is "jealous of the baby." To Linh, who is worn out, it looks like she has failed him. To the school, it looks like something to be assessed again.

**What is true.** He is not withdrawing from people, and he is not jealous of Mai. He is **saving himself.** Every ordinary thing now costs more than he has, so he has stopped doing the things that cost the most. Circle time is loud. He has nothing left for it.

**The truth about Mai.** He loves her. When she cries in the night he does not mind the sound. He minds that it will not stop and he does not know when it will. In the dark, once, he went into her room and stood next to her crib until she was quiet. He did not tell anyone. This is the one thing in the story that is not on anyone's radar. Only Ông has seen it.

**The adults' truth.** His parents have done almost everything right. Nobody knows how loud the nights have become for him, because he cannot say it, and because he is so quiet that they thought he was coping.

## 7. What Tam believes

- **If he is quiet enough, it will pass.** This is what has worked all his life, so he is quieter.
- **A thing he is in the middle of should be finished.** Stopping early feels like being wrongly cut off.
- **Sound is a thing that happens to him.** He has never been given a way to say "turn it down."
- **Mai is his to look after.** He took the job without anyone asking.
- **Nobody has asked him what would help,** and he is not sure it is something that could be asked.

## 8. What each person knows about him

- **Má (Linh):** he is not himself. She thinks it is her fault, and she is too tired to say so. She is afraid the school is right, and afraid they are wrong in the way they were before.
- **Ba (Dũng):** Tam is "just quiet." He is sure it will pass, and he is not sure.
- **Ông:** Tam is fine, and Tam is not fine. Ông has seen him standing by the crib.
- **His teacher:** he stopped joining circle time and goes under the table.
- **Isla:** a child evaluated at four, a school referral, and parents who are loving and exhausted. **Isla does not know about the noise or about the crib.**

## 9. Isla and the session

This is Tam's **first session.** Nobody at Willow House has met him except on the intake call, which Linh took in English on her break and Dũng took the second half of in his own way. It is a **Tuesday in late winter,** because Ông can bring him on Tuesdays.

- **Ông brings him** and waits in the waiting room, with a newspaper he is not reading. Ông calls Isla *Cô*, as a child would a teacher, and does not shake her hand until she offers it. Má is asleep after a night shift.
- **The first minutes matter more than the book.** Tam does not come in until the room is right.
- Nothing is explained to him. Isla does not ask him anything. She makes the room quieter and waits.

## 10. Tam in the playroom

What he notices, avoids and does there. These are the clues the player can find. None are scored, and none are explained:

- **He stops at the door,** looks at the light, the window and the radiator, and does not come in.
- **He steps back** at the first knock of the radiator, and covers one ear with the heel of his hand.
- **He is bothered by the hiss** of the white-noise machine in the hall, and by the bright overhead light.
- **He goes under the low table** when the room is too much, and stays there.
- **He taps twice** on the table before he sits, and checks the window.
- **He lines up the animals** from the toy shelf, shortest to longest, and moves one that is out of place.
- **He hums** the train sound, and stops when the radiator knocks.
- **He takes out the book** from his backpack and puts it on the floor in front of Isla.
- **He turns back to the same page.**
- **He puts a toy in Isla's hand** and walks away.

## 11. Tam's things

- **The book:** *The Little Train Over the Bridge*, a worn picture book, the corners soft, with a sticker of Ông's on the spine so it can always be found. He carries it in his backpack and has since he was four. He wants it read the same way every time, in the same voice, from the first page, and he turns back to one page, where the train crosses the bridge. Isla recognizes this: it is the one book that matters.
- **His backpack,** on both shoulders.
- **The train car:** a small orange wooden car, one wheel missing, always in his pocket. He gives it to people he trusts, for a moment, and takes it back.
- **A pair of headphones,** the big soft kind, which he does not wear at Willow House, because nobody has asked.

## 12. The secrets, as discoveries **(decision)**

Each is something the player *finds*, by changing the room or by noticing, before Tam does anything about it. He never tells.

1. **The noise.** The radiator knock and the white-noise hiss hurt. Isla notices him cover his ear at the knock, and goes into the hall to turn the machine down.
2. **The light.** The overhead light is wrong, and the lamp is right. Lower the light and he comes out from under the table.
3. **The book.** He has brought his own. Isla must not offer another, and must read his book, again, the same way.
4. **The word.** After enough of all this, he says one word, once, softly, to the book and not to Isla: *Mai.* It is not what the adults thought. It means he is thinking about his sister, not resenting her.

The adult meaning is never said aloud. The player puts it together.

## 13. How he communicates

Rules for every line and every action:
- **Almost nothing is said.** Single words, rarely, in one language or the other. The text shows what he does, in a few plain sentences.
- **He answers with objects and position:** a toy put in a hand, a book placed in front of someone, where he sits, whether he comes in.
- **He repeats.** The same page, the same motion, the same hum.
- **He does not answer questions,** and the game must not make a question the right move.
- **He is not rude, absent or sad.** He is busy being a person in a loud world.
- **The narrator describes behavior and never feelings.** "He covers one ear with the heel of his hand," not "the sound hurts him."

| What it means | What Tam would do |
|---|---|
| "It's too loud." | Covers one ear. Steps back to the door. |
| "Turn it down." | Looks at the machine in the hall, then at Isla. |
| "I need to be under here." | Goes under the table and taps twice. |
| "Read it again." | Turns back the page. Says, once, *nữa.* |
| "I trust you a little." | Puts the orange train car in her hand, and takes it back a moment later. |
| "I love my sister." | Says *Mai,* once, to the book. |

## 14. The book problem

- **The fit:** *The Little Train Over the Bridge,* the book he brings. It is read the same way each time. A short story: a small train goes over a long bridge, and the bridge hums. It ends with the train on the other side and the bridge quiet. Nothing is explained.
- **The shallow choice:** a train book from Willow House's shelf. He looks at it and sets it down.
- **The trap:** *A New Baby at Our House,* from the shelf. It names the thing he is bearing, and he goes under the table and stays.

## 15. The act and its ending

The door and the light, the radiator, the machine in the hall, the lamp, the book from his bag read more than once, the one word, a closing, and then feedback.

Nothing is announced. The feedback comes from Ông, who walks him out, and later from his mother. The ending is a small thing Tam does: he leaves the orange train car on the table for Isla, or he does not, depending on what she held.

## 16. Open questions

1. **Is the word *Mai* right,** or too neat? It says the thing everyone misread, in one word. The alternative is *nữa,* which is true but smaller.
2. **How much of the crib scene belongs in the game?** It could stay entirely in the novel.
3. **Does the practical fix feel like an ending?** The feedback is a small change: headphones, a quieter corner, a door left closed at night. It is not a cure.
4. **Is "first session" the right moment,** or would the third session, like Wren's, give the player more to build on?
5. **What does Linh say in her message afterward** that the player should feel the weight of?
6. **How do we avoid a "special needs" story,** where the child exists to teach the adult? He should be a person first: funny, exact, a little bossy about his trains.
7. **How do we get the Vietnamese right?** Words, kinship terms, food and the new-year details should be checked by a reader from the community, and the family's English should read as a person's English, not a type's.
8. **How much of the Lunar New Year belongs in this episode?** It adds real noise and real love, and it could crowd the session. It might sit only in the notes and in the transition card.
