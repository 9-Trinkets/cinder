## 2. Key Cultural Phrases & Code-Switching

In the house, the primary language is **Mandarin Chinese**, but characters regularly code-switch into Japanese, Korean, and English depending on their comfort level, emotion, or who they are addressing.

### Taiwanese Mandarin Colloquialisms & Particles
* **真的假的 (Zhēn de jiǎ de?)**: *"Seriously? For real?"* Universal Taiwanese conversation starter used constantly by Mio and Kai.
* **誇張 (Kuāzhāng)**: *"That's insane / ridiculous / over the top."* Used to react to everything from prices to drama.
* **蛤 (Hā / Há?)**: The reflexive Taiwanese exclamation when surprised or when asking someone to repeat themselves.
* **辛苦了 (Xīnkǔ le)**: *"Thank you for your hard work."* Said when someone finishes cooking, returns from a long shoot, or clears the table.
* **麻煩你了 (Máfán nǐ le)**: *"Sorry to trouble you / appreciate you doing this."* Polite, slightly distancing phrase.
* **沒事啦 (Méi shì la)**: *"It's nothing / don't worry about it."* Kai and Mio’s go-to deflecting phrase when hurt.
* **吃飽沒？ (Chī bǎo méi?)**: Taiwanese Hokkien/Mandarin greeting: *"Have you eaten yet?"* Expresses hospitality and care.

### Japanese Cultural Cues & Kitchen Terminology
* **いただきます (Itadakimasu)**: Said before meals. Ren says this quietly every single time, even when eating convenience store toast.
* **ごちそうさまでした (Gochisousama deshita)**: *"Thank you for the meal."* Always said to Ren by Daichi and Aera after dinner.
* **お疲れ様です (Otsukaresama desu)**: Used between Ren and Daichi when crossing paths late at night.
* **確かに (Tashika ni)**: *"Indeed / That makes sense."* Daichi's favorite agreement filler when analyzing a situation.
* **仕込み (Shikomi)**: Kitchen prep work. Ren's sanctuary—his 6:00 AM mise-en-place routine.
* **こだわり (Kodawari)**: Obsessive, uncompromising dedication to craft or quality. Defines Ren's cooking philosophy.

### Korean Expressions & Emotional Markers
* **대박 (Daebak)**: *"Incredible / Wow."* Slipped out by Aera when Mio shows her fashion designs or when food is surprisingly good.
* **진짜 (Jinjja?)**: *"Really?"* Aera's quiet reflex when listening intently.
* **괜찮아요 (Gwaenchanh-ayo)**: *"I'm okay / It doesn't matter."* Aera's reflexive defensive phrase when she is actually overwhelmed.
* **오빠 / 언니 (Oppa / Unnie)**: Aera rarely uses these in public Mandarin, but when speaking Korean on late-night phone calls back to Seoul, the shift in tone reveals her vulnerability.
