# Game Concept: AERA

> *"Polite people make for terrible television. If they aren't bleeding a little by episode two, the network pulls the plug."*

---

## 1. High Concept & Overview
* **Title**: *Aera* (or *AERA: The Pilot Season*)
* **Protagonist / Player Role**: The Showrunner / Field Producer stationed in the on-site "Video Village" control room.
* **Genre**: Reality TV Simulation × Workplace Social Engineering × Psychological Drama.
* **Inspirations**: *Terrace House*, *UnREAL*, Joan Didion's observational essays, Kazuo Ishiguro's detached emotional restraint.
* **Core Themes**:
  - The architecture of manufactured vulnerability vs. authentic human connection.
  - Voyeurism, media complicity, and the ethical compromises of entertainment.
  - How environment, scarcity, and quiet social pressure alter human psychology.

---

## 2. The Narrative Voice & Novelization Tone
In both the game's text and its literary novelization, the story is told in the **first-person perspective ("I")** of the show's field producer. 

The narrator is not a cartoon villain; they are an expert diagnostician of human insecurity. They sit in the 66-degree chill of the control truck, headset clamped over one ear, drinking stale drip coffee, watching sixteen video feeds simultaneously. They genuinely like these four young people—yet their career and livelihood depend on engineering moments where their psychological armor cracks.

### Excerpt from the Producer's Notebook:
> *The control room was kept at sixty-six degrees to keep the tape decks and switcher racks from sweating, which meant I lived in a fleece jacket even in August.*
>
> *On Monitor 4—the tight lens mounted above the kitchen range hood—Ren was slicing ginger into paper-thin ribbons. His posture was clinical, shoulders squared, knife moving with that effortless, quiet metronome of someone who had survived three years on a French line in Roppongi. On Monitor 7, in the lounge, Mio was sitting cross-legged on the beige sectional, pretending to read an art magazine while watching the kitchen reflection in the switched-off TV screen.*
>
> *“Audio,” I murmured into the headset. “Check Ren’s lav. I’m getting fabric rustle on the right channel.”*
> *“Clear on two,” the mixer’s voice crackled back.*
>
> *I tapped the call sheet against my knee. On it were three index cards the network exec had texted me at seven this morning: halve the grocery budget, force a house meeting, or send someone into the kitchen to interrupt.*
>
> *Left to their own devices, these four would spend the next two weeks being polite, bowing slightly at hallway crossings, and eating separately in their bedrooms. The pilot would die on the editing floor before episode two.*
>
> *“Chika,” I pressed the comms switch to the floor PA. “Tell Mio that Ren bought oat milk because he remembered she liked it in her tea. Don’t tell her I told you. Just let it drop.”*

## 3. The Central Workplace Dynamic: The Production Crew

Rather than operating abstract user interfaces, the player interacts with a dedicated **three-person production crew** that represents the different pressures of unscripted television:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        THE CONTROL TRUCK CREW                          │
├────────────────────┬────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┤
│  MAYA (Network)    │  CHIKA (Floor PA)  │  TOBY (Lead Editor)          │
│  "Give me numbers  │  "I'm on the floor │  "I can make anyone look     │
│   or we're done."  │   with the kids."  │   like a saint or a monster."│
└────────────────────┴────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Maya (Network Liaison / Executive Producer)**:
   * Demands ratings, high demographic engagement, and commercial viability.
   * Hands down the **Morning Mandate** and **Event Cards**.
2. **Chika (Floor PA / Field Producer)**:
   * Wears sneakers and a headset behind the two-way mirrors; actually talks to the cast between takes.
   * Radios in the **Afternoon Situation Report** and offers tactical **Relationship Nudges**.
3. **Toby (Senior Video Editor)**:
   * Cynical, sleep-deprived veteran who treats human heartache purely as rhythm and timeline pacing.
   * Conducts the night Confessionals and assembles the competing **Broadcast Cuts** for your final sign-off.

## 4. The Cast: Four Personalities Under Pressure

The housemates are carefully selected archetypes with pre-existing insecurities designed to grate or resonate against each other:

| Housemate | Age / Role | Public Persona | Hidden Wound / Trigger |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Aera** | 24, Doc Film Editor | Quiet, perceptive, minimalist, vegan. Observes everything; emotionally self-contained. | Terrified of being perceived as cold or unlovable; retreats and freezes under direct emotional confrontation. |
| **Ren** | 27, Bistro Chef | Grounded, competent, acts of service. Expresses care exclusively through kitchen labor. | Secretly insecure about his lack of formal pedigree; takes kitchen critiques as deep personal rejections. |
| **Mio** | 22, Fashion Student / Model | Charming, quick-witted, magnetic, trend-conscious. Effortlessly commands attention. | Desperate for validation beyond her physical appearance; deeply triggered when treated as shallow or disposable. |
| **Daichi** | 26, Startup Ops / Triathlete | Disciplined, hyper-structured, routine-driven, polite. Dislikes unstructured downtime. | Low emotional vocabulary; panics and withdraws when forced into ambiguous, messy romantic confrontations. |

## 5. Potential Pilot Endings
At the end of Day 2, the player assembles the final broadcast cut and receives the network verdict:

* **Ending A: The Greenlit Sensation (High Ratings, High Connection)**
  * The network orders a full 12-episode season. A genuine, hard-won romantic or platonic bond formed between two housemates amidst the chaos.
* **Ending B: The Tabloid Trainwreck (High Ratings, Low Safety)**
  * Massive online buzz, but at extreme human cost. An actor walks out or suffers a tearful breakdown on camera. The network celebrates; the producer carries the guilt.
* **Ending C: The Boring Pilot (Low Ratings, High Safety)**
  * Everyone was respectful, polite, and safe. The footage is gentle, peaceful, and utterly unbroadcastable. The show is quietly shelved.
* **Ending D: The Director's Resignation (The Walkout)**
  * The producer refuses to air the most compromising footage, protecting the cast's privacy and sacrificing their own television career.
